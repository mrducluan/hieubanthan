## 1. Brief
Before writing: intent signature, primary keyword, outline (each H2/H3 answers one real question), table to build, entities and sources, internal links, and 3 SERP gaps (verified or marked `unverified`). Length follows the intent: answer every question in the pool and stop. There is no word target; a thin page padded to 2,000 words ranks worse than a tight 900-word page.

## 2. Page structure
1. H1 with the primary keyword, written for a person.
2. Opening scene: a concrete situation or dilemma, 2-4 sentences. No definition opener.
3. First H2 phrased as the main question, immediately followed by the bold direct answer.
4. Body H2/H3, each answering one question and understandable without the rest of the page (answer engines lift single passages).
5. Comparison table.
6. Cross-system bridge section.
7. What to do: concrete steps for work, relationships or money.
8. FAQ (3-5), then Sources.

## 3. Direct answer block
Immediately under the first H2, one paragraph, entirely bold, 40-60 words, self-contained (no "as above"), naming the entity, the condition and the practical conclusion. It serves featured snippets and AI summaries. Nothing guarantees extraction, but a clean block is the cheapest thing to lift.

## 4. Comparison table
At least one real Markdown table with two or more variables (for example Đắc địa vs Hãm địa x Tuần vs Triệt, or natal house vs transit effect). Cells hold short, differentiated statements, not repeated adjectives. Add a one-sentence takeaway under it.

## 5. Cross-system bridge
One H2/H3 that sets an Eastern mechanism beside a Western one by shared life-stage theme (for example the age-30 consolidation period in both). State it as an **analogy**, name what differs (calendar, method, what is measured), and never claim the two systems produce the same effect. Keep it to about 20% of the page. Western facts that can be stated: Saturn's orbital period is about 29.5 years, so a first return falls near ages 28-30 and a second near 57-60.

## 6. Voice and rhythm
Knowledgeable but warm: precise terms, then plain words. Mix short sentences (6-10 words) with longer analytical ones (up to about 35). Avoid stock openers and transitions ("Trong bức tranh toàn cảnh", "Hãy cùng khám phá", "Tóm lại,", "Không chỉ ... mà còn"); the validator checks them. Prefer a specific example to an adjective.

## 7. Experience, sources, safety
- **First-hand value:** add something a generic rewrite cannot: a worked example on a sample chart, an anonymised case with consent, a decision checklist, or the editor's observed pattern. Label what is classical teaching and what is editorial judgment.
- **Sources:** name the text (*Tử Vi Đẩu Số Toàn Thư*, *Uyên Hải Tử Bình*, an astronomy source for cycles). Link primary or authoritative pages. Paraphrase; quote at most one line.
- **Author/reviewer:** only real people who actually wrote or reviewed it. Otherwise credit the editorial team.
- **Safety:** no guarantees ("chắc chắn ly hôn"), no disease names or diagnoses, no dates of death, no lottery or gambling, no selling remedies or talismans. For health themes add that it does not replace a doctor. If a user may be in crisis, point to professional help instead of a reading.

## 8. FAQ
Three to five edge-case questions that real people ask and the page body did not cover. Each answer 40-80 words, begins with the answer, no marketing. Mark up as `###` question + paragraph.

## 9. Vietnamese conventions
Choose one spelling for the whole site (hoà/hòa, thuỷ/thủy) and keep it. Explain a Hán-Việt term on first use. Say clearly when a date is lunar or solar and which calendar rule is used. Slugs and URLs have no diacritics; titles and body keep them.
